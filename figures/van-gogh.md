# Van Gogh: a second case of compression and perceptual lag

Van Gogh confirms the Beethoven operator outside music, and especially its fifth element, perceptual lag (see [beethoven.md](beethoven.md)).

- **Two operators, one step removed.** The sun is the source; the sunflower is its earthly double. The viewer perceives the sun through the flower. In *The Sower* (1888) a huge yellow sun disk stands against violet: two complementary operators with energy running between them.
- **Compression in color.** Among saturated hues, yellow appears the most luminous. *Sunflowers* (Arles, 1888) set yellow on yellow, compressing value contrast so energy is held in hue and thick impasto. Small complementary accents provide the release path.
- **His own words.** He wrote to Theo that reaching the "high yellow note" of that summer meant keeping himself keyed up, a compression image in musical terms. In Nuenen he took piano lessons, comparing notes to colors.
- **Death becoming light.** He described *Wheatfield with a Reaper* (1889) as an image of death with nothing sad in it, under a sun flooding everything with pure gold: the Egmont structure, and Shakespeare's paired affects in one condition.

## Where the parallel differs

Beethoven's eruption unfolds in time; a painting is seen at once, so Van Gogh's step happens in the eye's path across the canvas or across a series. Beethoven compressed through slow, reductive sketching; Van Gogh often painted fast, alla prima, compressing in the act of painting, though he repeated the *Sunflowers* several times.
