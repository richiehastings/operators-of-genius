# Transmission: Furtwängler and concealed control

The performer is a fourth node in the chain: the shortened path must be rebuilt at every performance.

## Evidence

- **Improvisatory shaping.** Furtwängler wanted the work to sound as if born in the moment. He traded some note-level precision for the energy of the whole.
- **The whole and long-range hearing.** From Schenker he took *Fernhören*: hearing the eruption pages in advance so every compressed moment already carries its release. Schenker credited genius with "the gift of improvisation and long-range hearing."
- **Tension and release as the rhythm of life.** Furtwängler described music's alternation of tension and relaxation as life's own rhythm: the Langer–Clynes isomorphism.
- **Tonality as natural law within the soul.** For him, tonality is the pathway between the physics of sound and inner life; atonality cuts it.
- **Schenker's procreative image.** Schenker described music's origin as a procreative motion entering man through the sonority Nature preordained in his hearing: an older theoretical ancestor of the Scriabin reading.

## The four-node chain

| Node | Role | Effort required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Energy in nature, life, the dynamic form of experience | None; it exists |
| Composer | Encodes the dynamic form into a work | Maximum precision, made invisible |
| Performer | Re-creates the work as if born now | Long-range hearing, improvisatory shaping |
| Listener | Takes in the form through the physics of hearing | Receives energy before recognizing the subject |

**Meta-model.** An unmediated experience is not given; it is constructed anew at each transmission, by control placed at the level of the whole rather than the detail.

## Concealed control

The artist controls the energy fully while keeping the next move hidden. The recipient trusts the direction without predicting the moment. Announcing the intention turns experience into information and adds a step to the path (Meyer 1956; Huron 2006).

Examples: Furtwängler's attack; Scriabin's false climaxes; the scherzo's return in the finale of Beethoven's Fifth.

*Attributed, source unverified:* Furtwängler is said to have remarked, on the audience's controlled excitement, that you would not tell your lover what you are going to do next. Possible sources: Elisabeth Furtwängler, *Über Wilhelm Furtwängler* (1979); the documentary *Furtwängler's Love*; his *Notebooks 1924–1954*.
