# Originality, open questions and next steps

**What appears new.** A shared operator grammar applied to every figure; physics used as structure across all three, not only for Shakespeare as in Rabkin; and the singular/collective interchange as an independent analytical axis.

## Falsifiability tests to define

- [ ] Mozart: what would a work look like that has many participants but no closure-forcing boundary, and does Mozart write any?
- [ ] Beethoven: find movements with heavy compression and no eruption. Are they rare, or do they form a distinct type?
- [ ] Perceptual lag: find Beethoven eruptions that do not move from low to middle register, and Van Gogh works with full value contrast and no compressed field. Do they read as weaker eruptions?
- [ ] Shakespeare: list peak moments with a pure, unmixed affect and check whether the play marks each as a limitation.
- [ ] Shakespeare: test the conservation principle against comedies with no excluded remainder.

## Open questions

- [ ] Is the conversational operator unique to Mozart, or a general Classical mechanism he used most fully? Test against Haydn.
- [ ] Does Beethoven's eruption correspond to the Shakespearean soliloquy at a tragic climax?

## Candidate next figures

Haydn (common Classical root), Bach and Newton (systematic, completing geniuses), Beethoven and Michelangelo (struggle against the medium).
