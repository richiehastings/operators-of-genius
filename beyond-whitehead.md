# Beyond Whitehead: sensing science

**Hypothesis (Richard).** Whitehead knew that the philosophy of sensing and perceiving was insufficiently developed in the logical philosophy of the 1890s–1930s, but lacked the sensory-device science that now exists to synthesize with it. For this reason he seemed unimpressed by Wittgenstein.

## The Wittgenstein evidence

- In a 1963 letter to Joseph Levinson, Russell wrote that as far as he knew, Whitehead never read Wittgenstein, then recounted a tea at the Whiteheads' house where Wittgenstein behaved in his usual dominating way (*Dear Bertrand Russell*, p. 159).
- **Cautions.** On this evidence the reaction was personal, not a reasoned critique of the *Tractatus*; and Russell is known to "improve" his Wittgenstein anecdotes in retelling.
- **The documented philosophical gap.** Wittgenstein made language the boundary of thought: the *Tractatus* ends by passing over in silence what cannot be said. Whitehead treated language as the inadequate tool: *Process and Reality* holds that weak insight and deficient language block any final statement of first principles, so philosophy must keep stretching language toward experience.

## What Whitehead lacked (he died in 1947)

| Development | Date | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information theory (Shannon) | 1948 | Mathematics of signal, noise and transmission |
| Cybernetics (Wiener) | 1948 | Feedback, control, systems that sense and respond |
| Single-cell recording of perception (Hubel & Wiesel) | from 1959 | How sensory signals are built into structured perception |
| Sensor and signal-processing technology | ongoing | Transducers for sound, light, pressure, chemistry, analyzed at scale |
| Predictive processing (Friston, Clark and others) | 2000s– | Perception as prediction corrected by incoming signals |

## Mapping onto the two modes of perception

| Whitehead | Modern sensing science |
| --- | --- |
| Causal efficacy | Bodily, interoceptive, low-resolution signals: bass pressure, arousal, heartbeat |
| Presentational immediacy | High-resolution, predicted, structured perception: the clear image, the identified melody |
| Symbolic reference | Predictive processing: the brain's model meeting the body's signals |

The synthesis this series pursues: Whitehead's metaphysics plus present-day sensing and perception science, applied through the operators. A system that takes in raw signals and builds structured perception operates between the same two modes; the framework describes what an artistic signal does on that path.
