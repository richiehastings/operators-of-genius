# The operator grammar

Each type of genius is described by one generative operator, stated in the same four terms so different figures can be compared formally rather than by impression. Under the [thesis](thesis.md), each operator describes how one translator handles a common physical dynamic. The series so far covers Mozart, Beethoven (with Van Gogh as a second case), Shakespeare, Scriabin (bypass) and Furtwängler (transmission).

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Domain (D) | The bounded space the work happens in: a form, a tonal frame, a stage |
| Participants (P) | The agents inside it: themes paired with instruments, characters, affects |
| Operator (C) | How participants act on each other: exchange, compression, opposition, enactment |
| Closure | The condition under which the work may end, and what kind of ending it produces |

The framework draws on physics as structure, not metaphor: equilibrium (Mozart), phase transition (Beethoven), complementarity and conservation (Shakespeare), path shortening (Scriabin).

## Meta-model: transmission

The dynamic passes through four nodes: source, composer, performer, listener. An unmediated experience is not given; it is constructed anew at each transmission, by control placed at the level of the whole rather than the detail.

- **Perceptual lag** (Beethoven, Van Gogh): energy is stored where perception is weakest and released where it is strongest.
- **Bypass** (Scriabin, Van Gogh): the perceived path from source to perceiver is shortened by maximizing the precision of the actual transformations.
- **Concealed control** (Furtwängler): the trajectory is fully controlled while the next move is withheld; announcing it turns experience into information and lengthens the path.

In Whitehead's terms ([whitehead.md](whitehead.md)), all three mechanisms govern the passage between *causal efficacy* (felt, bodily force) and *presentational immediacy* (clear, distinct display). Each new operator should meet his four criteria: coherence, logic, applicability, adequacy.
