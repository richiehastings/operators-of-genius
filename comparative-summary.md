# Comparative summary

Mozart distributes energy into equilibrium, Beethoven concentrates and releases it, and Shakespeare splits it into conserved opposites across scale.

| Element | Mozart | Beethoven | Shakespeare |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domain | Fixed boundary that contains | Elastic boundary, finally breached | Stage and audience as two scales |
| Participants | Many distinct characters | One kernel as protagonist | Paired affects, singular and collective |
| Operator | Conversation and exchange | Compression and accumulation | Opposition and recursive decay |
| Critical event | Ensemble: all voices at once | Eruption: one singular expression | Concurrent joy and sorrow in one condition |
| Closure | Reconciliation or absorption | Triumph or transcendence | Conservation: joy with a remainder |
| Process | Generative model, few drafts | Reductive struggle, massive sketches | Transformation of borrowed sources, revision |
| Physics analogue | Equilibrium | Phase transition | Complementarity and conservation |
