# Comparative summary

Mozart distributes energy into equilibrium, Beethoven concentrates and releases it, Shakespeare splits it into conserved opposites across scale, and Scriabin shortens its path to the perceiver.

| Element | Mozart | Beethoven | Shakespeare | Scriabin (bypass) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domain | Fixed boundary that contains | Elastic boundary, finally breached | Stage and audience as two scales | Continuous single span, no movements |
| Participants | Many distinct characters | One kernel as protagonist | Paired affects, singular and collective | Self and cosmos, fused |
| Operator | Conversation and exchange | Compression and accumulation | Opposition and recursive decay | Enactment of dynamic form |
| Critical event | Ensemble: all voices at once | Eruption: one singular expression | Concurrent joy and sorrow in one condition | Successive waves, final climax |
| Closure | Reconciliation or absorption | Triumph or transcendence | Conservation: joy with a remainder | Fusion ("I am") |
| Process | Generative model, few drafts | Reductive struggle, massive sketches | Transformation of borrowed sources, revision | Maximal mediation made invisible |
| Physics analogue | Equilibrium | Phase transition | Complementarity and conservation | Path shortening |
| Invariant governed | Ratio relations, participant count | Energy contour | Number of opposed forces | Path length |

Furtwängler is not a fifth column: he operates at the performer node of the transmission chain, rebuilding whichever operator a work carries (see [figures/furtwangler.md](figures/furtwangler.md)).
